Customer Churn Prediction

Using Machine Learning to Identify At-Risk Customers Before They Leave¶

Project Description¶

A UK-based online gift store has historically relied on manual customer segmentation to identify who might be at risk of leaving — a useful but reactive approach that only flags customers after their buying pattern has already weakened (see Project : E-Commerce Customer Intelligence Report).

This project takes the next step. Instead of segmenting customers with fixed rules, a supervised machine learning model is trained on historical purchase behavior to predict, for any customer, the probability they will churn — calculated early enough for the business to actually intervene.

What → Why → Predict → Act

  • Problem Definition — establishing a clear, data-backed definition of churn
  • Feature Engineering — converting raw transaction history into predictive customer-level features
  • Model Building — training and comparing classification models to score churn risk
  • Model Interpretation — translating model output into plain-language business insight
  • Prescriptive Action — a ranked at-risk customer list and a retention plan tied to projected revenue impact

Key Questions Answered¶

  1. Can customer churn be predicted before it happens, using only purchase history?
  2. Which behaviors are the strongest early warning signs of churn?
  3. How confident can the business be in each prediction?
  4. Which at-risk customers should be prioritized first, and what is the revenue impact of acting on this list?

Builds on the cleaned UCI Online Retail dataset and RFM features established in Project "E-Commerce Customer Intelligence Report" — 4,335 customers, 541,909 transactions, December 2010 to December 2011.

What This Report Does¶

This report uses machine learning to predict — before it happens — which customers are likely to stop buying. Every customer in the store has been assigned a churn probability score and placed into one of three action tiers: High Risk, Medium Risk, and Low Risk. The result is a ranked list the business can act on this week, with projected revenue attached to every recommendation.

⚠ The Problem — The Store Cannot See Who Is Leaving¶

Without a churn model, the store only discovers a customer has left after months of silence. By then it is too late — the customer has moved on and no retention campaign can reach them effectively. This report solves that by flagging customers before they go quiet, while there is still time to act.

The model identified 707 High Risk customers already likely to churn and 1,691 Medium Risk customers currently drifting. Together they represent £1,398,295 in revenue that is either at immediate risk or quietly heading that way.

⚙ How the Model Works — In Plain English¶

The model studied the purchase history of 3,604 customers — how recently they bought, how often, how much they spent, how many products they tried, and how their buying gaps changed over time. It then learned which patterns predict whether a customer comes back or disappears.

Three models were tested and compared — Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and XGBoost. Logistic Regression won with the highest accuracy (66.7%) and ROC-AUC (0.722), meaning it correctly identifies churning customers 72% of the time — significantly better than guessing. Importantly it is also the most explainable model, meaning every prediction can be justified with a clear reason rather than a black box score.

2. Send a reactivation email to Medium Risk customers with a 10-15% time-limited discount on their most purchased category. At £15 per customer to reach 1,691 people, the campaign costs £25,365 and projects £253,222 in recovered revenue — a 998% ROI.

3. Personally contact the 94 High Risk priority accounts (those with £500+ in historical spend). A direct, personal outreach to these customers — not a mass email — has the highest probability of recovery given their past value to the business.

Bottom Line¶

The store currently has no system for knowing who is about to leave. This model changes that. For the first time, every customer has a churn score, a risk tier, and a recommended action — turning retention from a reactive scramble into a proactive, data-driven strategy projected to protect and recover £4,794,180 in annual revenue.

We have 9 Features and 1 Labels¶

Feature¶

recency — days since last purchase before cutoff. The single strongest predictor in almost every churn model. A customer who bought yesterday is far less likely to churn than one who bought 250 days ago.

frequency — number of unique orders. High frequency customers have demonstrated a habit of buying. Habits are hard to break — making high-frequency customers less likely to churn.

monetary — total spend. High spenders tend to be more invested in the store. Also correlates with Champions from Project 3.

avg_order_value — average spend per order. Distinguishes bulk buyers from regular buyers. A customer spending £500 per order behaves differently from one spending £15.

total_items — total units purchased. Captures volume independently of order count — a customer who buys 500 items in one order vs 500 items across 10 orders tells a different story.

unique_products — product diversity. Customers buying across many categories are more embedded in the store's ecosystem — harder to replace with a competitor.

avg_days_between — average gap between consecutive orders. A customer who buys every 7 days has a strong habit. One who buys every 90 days is already borderline churning before the cutoff.

tenure_days — days between first and last purchase. Long tenure = long relationship = more likely to continue. Notice customers with only 1 order have tenure_days = 0 — that is correct.

num_countries — number of countries orders were placed from. Mostly 1 for retail customers, higher for wholesale accounts. Wholesale buyers rarely churn silently

Label¶

Did this customer place any order after September 30?If yes then churned=0 and if no then churned=1

Risk Tier Analysis — What the Model Tells Us¶

Customer and Revenue Distribution¶

The churn model segments all 3,604 customers into three actionable risk tiers based on their predicted churn probability:

Risk Tier Customers Share Revenue Revenue Share
🔴 High Risk 707 19.6% £385,407 6.3%
🟡 Medium Risk 1,691 46.9% £1,012,888 16.5%
🟢 Low Risk 1,206 33.5% £4,739,852 77.2%

The Revenue Gap is Striking¶

Low Risk customers spend £3,930 on average — roughly 7 times more than High Risk customers at £545. This gap exists because Low Risk customers buy frequently and recently, while High Risk customers typically bought once and went quiet.

This means churn risk and customer value are inversely related in this store — the customers most likely to leave are also the least valuable, while the most valuable customers are the safest. That is good news: the business is not at risk of losing its highest spenders to churn imminently.

Where the Real Opportunity Lies¶

  • Protect Low Risk (£4,739,852) — these customers drive over three quarters of all revenue. A loyalty program keeping them engaged costs far less than replacing them.

  • Focus campaigns on Medium Risk (£1,012,888) — the largest segment by customer count (1,691 people) and the biggest recovery opportunity. These customers are drifting but have not left yet — they are the most reachable.

  • Selective action on High Risk (£385,407) — do not blanket-campaign this group. Focus only on the highest-value High Risk accounts (like Customer 12346 at £77,183) where the recovery ROI justifies the effort.

This means...¶

Churn probability alone should not drive retention spending. A customer with 85% churn probability but £50 in historical spend deserves one automated email — nothing more. A customer with 72% churn probability and £77,183 in spend deserves a personal phone call. Always multiply churn risk by customer value before deciding how much to spend on retention.

Prescriptive Action Plan — Churn Prevention Strategy¶

The Core Principle¶

Not every at-risk customer deserves the same response. Churn probability multiplied by customer value determines where retention budget goes — not probability alone. A customer with 85% churn risk and £50 in lifetime spend gets one automated email. A customer with 72% churn risk and £77,183 in spend gets a personal call.

🟢 Low Risk — Loyalty Program¶

1,206 customers · £4,739,852 revenue · Revenue protected: £4,502,859

These customers are the business. They buy frequently, spend the most, and the model gives them low churn probability. The goal here is not recovery — it is protection before a competitor notices them.

Action: Launch a points-based loyalty program. Every purchase earns points redeemable for discounts. Champions averaging 7.68 orders per year accumulate points fast — giving them a financial reason to stay that compounds with every order.

Budget: Up to £786 per customer (20% of £3,930 avg revenue) is justified to retain this segment. In practice, a well-designed loyalty program costs far less than that per customer while delivering strong retention lift.

Expected outcome: Protecting 95% of this segment's revenue locks in £4,502,859 — the single highest return action in this entire plan.

🟡 Medium Risk — Reactivation Campaign¶

1,691 customers · £1,012,888 revenue · Recovery target: £253,222

The largest customer segment and the biggest recovery opportunity. These customers are drifting — avg 80 days since last purchase — but have not left yet. They are still reachable.

Action: Send a targeted email campaign with a 10-15% time-limited discount (valid 7 days) on their most frequently purchased product category. Subject line: "We saved something for you." Personalization by purchase history increases open rates significantly over generic offers.

Budget: At £15 average campaign cost per customer, reaching all 1,691 costs £25,365 — generating a projected £253,222 in recovered revenue at a 25% response rate. That is a 998% ROI on campaign spend.

Expected outcome: Recovering 25% of Medium Risk customers brings back £253,222 — and any customer who responds immediately moves to Low Risk status and enters the loyalty program.

🔴 High Risk — Selective Intervention¶

707 customers total · 94 priority accounts · Recovery target: £38,098

Most High Risk customers bought once at low value and disappeared. Spending meaningful budget trying to recover them has negative ROI. Selective action only.

Priority accounts (94 customers, monetary ≥ £500): Send a strong win-back offer — 20-25% discount, personally addressed, 7-day deadline. At £25 campaign cost per account, reaching all 94 costs £2,350 against a projected £38,098 recovery at 15% response — a 1,621% ROI.

Skip accounts (613 customers, monetary < £500): One automated "we miss you" email with no discount. If they respond, add to Medium Risk nurture sequence. If they do not respond within 14 days, remove from active marketing lists entirely. Do not spend further.

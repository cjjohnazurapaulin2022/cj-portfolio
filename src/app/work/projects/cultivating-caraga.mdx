## Executive Summary

Caraga Region's farmland decisions currently balance profit against food security with no formal framework — this project closes that gap using linear programming (a mathematical optimization method). The model determines the ideal split of farmland across five provinces and three major crops — rice, corn, and coconut — to maximize total farming profit, while guaranteeing rice and corn production never drops below 80% of today's levels, protecting the region's food supply. Built on real government production and cost data, the 21-constraint model identifies the allocation that generates the highest possible profit — **₱10.08 billion per cropping cycle** — without sacrificing food security. The result gives regional agricultural planners a data-backed, defensible starting point for land-use policy, rather than a purely profit-driven recommendation that risks the food supply.

## The Problem

Not every profitable choice is a good one. Coconut earns roughly 3x more profit per hectare than palay (rice) — so a model that only chases profit would push every province toward 100% coconut, crowding out the rice and corn that feed the region. The real question this project answers:

> How should Caraga allocate its farmland across palay, corn, and coconut — across five provinces — to maximize profit, while making sure rice and corn farming don't disappear?

Palay, corn, and coconut aren't an arbitrary shortlist — together they account for **~88% of all major-crop land** in Caraga, making them the crops that matter most to both the regional economy and food security.

## Approach

The work followed a five-step pipeline:

1. **Collect** — pulled real production, area, and cost data from the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) and Philippine Coconut Authority (PCA) across all five provinces.
2. **Calculate** — converted raw production numbers into yield-per-hectare and net-returns-per-hectare — the actual coefficients the model runs on.
3. **Build** — defined 15 decision variables (5 provinces × 3 crops) and a profit-maximizing objective function.
4. **Constrain** — added land limits, food-security floors, and realistic crop-share caps per province.
5. **Solve & Present** — solved the model with PuLP (CBC solver) in Python, validated the result, and translated it into a decision-ready allocation.

A first, unconstrained version of the model pushed every province to 100% coconut — a useful failure that confirmed why the food-security floors and growth caps were necessary, not optional.

## Tools & Methods

- **Python** — data processing and model orchestration
- **PuLP (CBC solver)** — linear programming formulation and solving
- **Jupyter Notebook** — analysis and validation
- **PSA / PCA datasets** — production, area, and cost data across all five Caraga provinces

## Key Findings & Results

- **Maximum total profit: ₱10.08 billion** per cropping cycle across all five provinces — the highest achievable profit under real-world constraints.
- **Food security fully protected** — palay and corn hold exactly at their 80% food-security floor in every province; production never drops below current levels.
- **Full land utilization** — every hectare of available farmland across all five provinces is allocated, with zero idle capacity.
- **Coconut absorbs all remaining land**, reaching its cap completely in Agusan del Sur — the model's most coconut-favorable province.
- **21 constraints in total** (5 land caps, 5 palay floors, 5 corn floors, 5 coconut caps, 1 non-negativity) keep the solution realistic rather than purely theoretical.

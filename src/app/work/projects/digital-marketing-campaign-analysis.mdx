## The Problem

A digital marketing team running campaigns across four channels — Social, Search, Email, and Display — needed answers to three critical questions their dashboard wasn't telling them:

- Which channels are actually generating returns, and which are consuming budget without proportional results?
- Which audience segments convert best, and are we spending enough to reach them?
- Is our Q4 revenue spike something we created with more spend — or organic seasonal demand we could be capitalizing on more?

Without answers, the team risks over-investing in underperforming channels, under-investing in high-efficiency ones, and missing the optimal window to push Q4 budget for maximum return.

## Dataset

300 campaigns · 12 months · 4 channels · 3 audience segments · 12 variables including spend, revenue, impressions, clicks, conversions, CTR, conversion rate, and ROI.

## Process

This analysis applies a three-layer analytical framework: **Descriptive** (what happened) → **Diagnostic** (why it happened) → **Prescriptive** (what to do).

**Data Cleaning & EDA**
The dataset was clean with zero null values and no duplicates across all 300 records. Key derived metrics — cost per conversion, revenue/spend ratio, and opportunity gap scores — were engineered in Pandas to power the diagnostic layer.

**Channel Performance Analysis**
Channels were ranked across CTR, conversion rate, ROI, and spend to identify structural performance gaps — not just surface-level averages.

**Segment × Channel Matrix**
A cross-tabulation of ROI and spend by all channel-segment combinations revealed opportunity gaps: combinations with high ROI but disproportionately low spend.

**Seasonal Decomposition**
Month-over-month revenue and spend growth rates were compared to determine whether Q4 peaks were spend-driven or demand-driven.

## Results

- **Search dominated ROI** at an average of 53.8x, followed by Email (31.3x), Social (23.2x), and Display (4.4x) — a gap wide enough to justify immediate reallocation
- **Email is the most underfunded channel** — it ranks #1 in conversion rate (4.95%) but receives the smallest budget (avg spend $4,547 vs Search's $13,726)
- **Display fails across every segment** — ROI ranges from just 0.25 to 8.5 across all three audiences, making it the clearest reallocation target in the portfolio
- **Email × Professionals is the #1 missed opportunity** — ROI of 63.3 on only $5,753 avg spend, with an opportunity gap score of +7 (highest in the dataset)
- **Q4 peaks are demand-driven, not spend-driven** — December revenue hit $23.13M on only $302K spend (76.5x ratio), with revenue growth of +52% on just +3% additional spend
- **Professionals are the most efficient audience** — highest conversion rate (5.17%), highest ROI (53.3), and lowest cost per conversion ($20.09 vs Seniors at $42.89)
- **Optimal Q4 launch window starts late September** — September showed +295% revenue growth on only +34% spend growth, meaning demand builds before Q4 officially starts

## Key Takeaways

- **Channel mix matters more than total budget.** Search and Email together justify increased investment; Display justified a cut across every single audience segment without exception.
- **The highest-converting channel was the most underfunded.** Email ranked #1 in conversion rate but received the least budget — a structural misallocation that compounded across all 12 months.
- **Q4 peaks are a demand window, not a spend window.** Revenue efficiency peaked in December at 76.5x — meaning the right strategy is to enter early (late September) and let demand do the work.
- **Segment targeting changes everything.** Professionals returned $20 per conversion vs $42 for Seniors — same channel, same spend, dramatically different outcomes based purely on audience selection.
